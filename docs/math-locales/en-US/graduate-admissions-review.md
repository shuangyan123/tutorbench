# Graduate-admission quantitative tests — reviewer guide (en-US)

The GRE General Test Quantitative Reasoning and the GMAT Exam are **not**
"postgraduate calculus tests." They must not inherit Chinese master's
entrance mathematics I/II/III, AP BC, or GRE Mathematics Subject Test
content assumptions.

## GRE General Quantitative Reasoning (ETS)

[ETS official Quantitative Reasoning content](https://www.ets.org/gre/test-takers/general-test/prepare/content/quantitative-reasoning.html)
focuses on arithmetic, algebra, geometry and data analysis. The GRE General
Quant section does **not** test calculus or trigonometry or require constructing
formal mathematical proofs.

Relevant authored knowledge: ratios, algebraic relations, geometry, probability
and data summaries. Synthetic Tutor checks such as `mc-percent-reversal`
and `mc-similarity-area` can be **adapted** to expose misunderstandings;
they are not ETS-authentic Quantitative Comparison or numeric-entry items.

**Missing:** quantitative comparison, multiple-answer/multiple-choice and
numeric-entry formats; context-aware estimation, answer choice constraints
and test-specific calculator policy.

## GMAT Exam (GMAC)

Use [official GMAT content](https://www.mba.com/exams/gmat-exam/about/exam-content)
and [exam structure](https://www.mba.com/exams/gmat-exam/about/exam-structure).
Treat **Quantitative Reasoning** and **Data Insights** as separate sections,
not one math score with one response format. Data Sufficiency evaluates
whether information is sufficient to determine an answer, which is not the
same as calculating the requested number.

The Quantitative Reasoning section does **not** allow a calculator, while
Data Insights provides an on-screen calculator. Review cases independently
for section-specific tool and timing assumptions.

- **Synthetic concept support:** `mc-linear-dependence` can prompt discussion
  of when equations are compatible, but the existing rank terminology is
  **not** an authentic GMAT prerequisite.
- **Data interpretation:** `mc-simpson-aggregation` provides a synthetic
  aggregation-reversal learning problem; it does **not** constitute a GMAT
  multi-source reasoning item.
- **Missing:** proper Data Sufficiency and Two-Part Analysis exam tasks, as
  well as official item-specific validity evidence.

## Shared core, different exam constraints

A mistake such as using the wrong percent base is portable mathematics.
Yet whether a Tutor should give an instant calculation, probe a misconception,
or teach a shortcut depends on the learner's goal and stage: learning,
practice, timed simulation, or post-exam review. No unobserved speed improvement
or score gain should be inferred.
