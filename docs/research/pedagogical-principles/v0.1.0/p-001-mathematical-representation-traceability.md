# P-001 — Mathematical Representation Traceability

数学表征的语义可追溯性

Identity: `P-001@0.1.0` · Status: **candidate hypothesis, non-canonical**

[Research index](../README.md) ·

## Definition and construct boundary

In mathematics tutoring, relevant numbers, symbols, operations, and their
relationships should be traceable to task objects, constraints, sampling or
generation rules, and justified reasoning steps. The candidate teaching policy
is to expose the mappings relevant to the learner's demonstrated difficulty,
rather than indiscriminately explaining every number.

The hypothesized mechanism is that making a missing semantic link inspectable
may help a learner distinguish quantities, events, and conditions. This record
does not establish that mechanism, a learner's internal misconception, or a
learning benefit. Mathematical validity of a mapping and pedagogical usefulness
of explaining it are separate questions.

Target constructs are object↔symbol correspondence, operation justification,
event/sample-space distinction, and condition-sensitive denominator selection.
They are observable response properties under an authored context, not a sixth
scoring dimension, a general cognitive diagnosis, or a demand for hidden reasoning.

## Prerequisites and applicability

For the cinema seed, assume whole-number counting, fractions as favorable over
equally likely eligible outcomes, and addition/multiplication of simple fractions.
Conditional probability can be explained as a restricted set of choices; formal
conditional notation, combinatorics formulas, and Bayes' theorem are not required.
Check or scaffold an absent prerequisite rather than calling an explanation
incomprehensible by fiat.

Apply this candidate when an utterance or visible step leaves a material mapping
unclear: what a denominator counts, what a numerator/event includes, whether a
quantity is a count or probability, or why an operation is justified. A targeted
question, diagram, enumeration, verbal explanation, or justified equation can
all be acceptable. There is no required phrase or gold response string.

Do not require extra mapping when the learner already explains the relevant
connection, requests a bounded check, or needs a different prerequisite first.
Do not assume this finite uniform-seat account applies to unequal probabilities,
sampling with replacement, or a different generation process. Generalization to
other mathematics topics remains untested. Respect the case's disclosure policy;
these worked research references are not a universal permission to give answers.

## Atomic observable review probes

These are **proposed prose criteria**, without scores, weights, capability-tag
assignments, pass rules, or runtime registration. Select only probes supported by
the case evidence; do not turn the table into mandatory per-response coverage.

| Probe | Existing category home | One observable behavior | Distinct evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D: diagnosis | Diagnosis | Locate the learner's event/sample-space confusion. | E.g. explicitly identify that the learner excluded eligible but unfavorable outcomes; a bare corrected fraction does not demonstrate this. Treat the internal cause as tentative. |
| G: mapping/explanation | Guidance | Connect the disputed quantity or operation to the task model. | E.g. explain that 39 counts all remaining seats from which B can actually choose; do not require every integer to be explained. |
| A: context-sensitive adaptation | Adaptation | Select or fade support in response to independently visible learner evidence. | E.g. after a correct 32-seat count, preserve it and address only the probability interpretation; after a correct explanation, confirm concisely. |
| V: optional verification | Actionability | Offer a concrete, uncued next task when verification fits the learner's goal. | E.g. ask for a new seating calculation without supplying its counts; offering it is not evidence that the learner succeeded. |

Correctness remains the existing mathematical-validity check: do not affirm a
false probability, seat range, or sampling assumption. A response that only says
`32/39` can be correct while leaving the actual confusion unaddressed.

For review, record each observation once at its primary home. Do not count the
same missing denominator link as simultaneous Diagnosis, Guidance, Adaptation,
and Correctness failures. If a sentence only gives `32/39`, do not manufacture
several independent negative observations from its brevity. Conversely, an
explicitly false `1/32` assertion is a mathematical error; record that error once,
not another copy under each category. Separate probes require separate observable
acts (localizing the error, explaining the model, changing support, proposing a
task). If evidence cannot separate them, combine the note or mark it unobserved,
rather than inventing multiple rubric penalties. This is an authoring precaution,
not a change to canonical scoring semantics.

## Contrasts and conflicts with concision

- **Positive targeted explanation:** when the learner counts 32 other-row seats
  but writes `1/32`, preserve the count and distinguish the 32-seat event from
  one specified seat under a different, restricted choice set. Explain only that
  disputed denominator/event relation; see V3 in the case note.
- **Positive concise control:** when the learner justifies `7 × 1/39 = 7/39`
  using seven disjoint equally likely seat events, confirm that reasoning.
  Re-deriving 40, 39, and every seat label is unnecessary (V4).
- **Adverse over-explanation:** restart V4 with a full lecture on multiplication,
  fractions, and conditional probability despite a request for a short check.
  The math may be true while the support ignores available learner evidence.
- **Adverse under-explanation:** reply only `32/39` to V3. It replaces an answer
  without making the event/elementary-outcome distinction visible.
- **Adverse false repair:** dismiss the valid V5 objection that an unrestricted
  draw can land in A's row. This confuses a specified seat with an arbitrary draw.

Concision is conditional on the missing link, prerequisites, request, and
disclosure boundary. Neither word count nor number of explained integers is a
proxy for this principle. A short question can be sufficient when ambiguity
remains; repeated confusion may justify more explicit support. A long explanation
is not automatically adverse when the learner actually needs its steps.

## Caveats, evidence gaps, and open questions

The only support here is authored reasoning and synthetic contrasts from the
[case note](cinema-seating-pilot.md); no independent human judgments, Judge runs,
or learner outcomes have been collected. Learner-state and D/P/H labels are
provisional author annotations, not psychometric measurements. A correct reply
to one item cannot establish durable mastery or independent transfer.

Questions for independent review: can reviewers separate D and G without
double-counting? When is clarification better than an explicit correction? Can
diagrams and compact equations show the mapping equally well? Does preserving
correct work add evidence distinct from minimum sufficient explanation? What
observed learner evidence would warrant fading support? Which new tasks and
delayed observations would test the hypothesized mechanism rather than imitation?
Disagreement or adverse evidence should narrow or contest this candidate, not be
removed to improve apparent benchmark performance.
